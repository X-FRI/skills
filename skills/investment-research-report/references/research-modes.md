# Research Modes

Use this reference before drafting. Select the closest research mode and let it shape evidence, valuation, and risk analysis.

## Single Company

Focus on:

- Business model and revenue drivers.
- Unit economics, margin structure, and free cash flow.
- Competitive advantage, customer concentration, and supplier risk.
- Balance sheet, capital allocation, dilution, dividends, and buybacks.
- Company-specific catalysts and governance risk.

Core question: can this company convert business performance into shareholder return at the current valuation?

## Industry or Sector

Focus on:

- Industry life cycle and recent change.
- Demand drivers and supply constraints.
- Value-chain profit pool and pricing power.
- Competitive concentration and leader share trend.
- Representative instruments: leaders, second-tier beta, ETFs, baskets, or upstream/downstream links.

Core question: which part of the industry can capture profit, and is that profit already priced in?

## Theme

Separate narrative from investable exposure.

Focus on:

- Beneficiaries versus visible but weak links.
- Timing, crowding, liquidity, and valuation elasticity.
- Near-term catalyst path.
- Whether the theme changes earnings, cash flow, or only attention.

Core question: what instrument actually monetizes the theme, and over what horizon?

## ETF or Basket

Focus on:

- Holdings concentration and top-weight exposure.
- Sector, factor, region, and currency exposure.
- Fees, liquidity, tracking error, and rebalancing behavior.
- Whether the ETF or basket gives the intended exposure more cleanly than single names.

Core question: is the instrument an efficient expression of the thesis?

## Cyclical or Resource

Focus on:

- Normalized earnings rather than peak earnings.
- Capacity cycle, inventory, utilization, and pricing.
- Commodity sensitivity, cost curve, reserve value, and production profile.
- Balance sheet survivability through the cycle.

Core question: where are we in the cycle, and what normalized economics should valuation use?

## High Growth or Temporarily Low Profit

Focus on:

- Unit economics and customer acquisition quality.
- Growth durability and path to margin.
- Dilution, stock-based compensation, and capital needs.
- Long-term margin assumptions and evidence required to support them.

Core question: does growth have a credible path to durable cash flow?

## Financials

Focus on:

- Balance sheet quality and asset-liability sensitivity.
- Net interest margin, credit cost, capital adequacy, and regulatory risk.
- Deposit or funding stability.
- Dividend sustainability and book value quality.

Core question: does the valuation reflect asset quality, capital risk, and sustainable return on equity?
